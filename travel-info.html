Visa & entry

Bhutan's visa rules and fees changed after 2022. We confirm the current requirements with every guest, and you can always check the official site, bhutan.travel. [VERIFY THIS SECTION AGAINST BHUTAN.TRAVEL BEFORE PUBLISHING]

Indian, Bangladeshi and Maldivian nationals

No visa is needed; you receive an entry permit at the port of entry. Indian nationals can use a passport or Voter ID card; others need a passport valid for at least six months. Booking through an operator isn't mandatory but is recommended. The Online Regional Permit System lets you arrange entry and route permits before arrival through registered tour operators or certified hotels, valid for entry via Paro and Phuentsholing.

All other nationalities

A visa must be cleared before you travel. Here's how it works with us:

  1. Choose your journey with us (we're a licensed Bhutanese operator).
  2. Send us a scan of your passport photo page.
  3. Pay the full trip cost, including the USD 40 visa fee, by wire transfer.
  4. We process your visa, usually within about 72 working hours of payment.
  5. Bring your visa clearance letter; the visa is stamped into your passport on arrival.

Costs & the Sustainable Development Fee

Bhutan's tourism policy is "High Value, Low Impact": visitor numbers have been managed since tourism opened in 1974 to keep it environmentally friendly, culturally respectful and economically fair.

Getting there

By air

Paro International Airport is Bhutan's only international airport. Drukair, the national carrier, flies to and from cities including Bangkok, Kathmandu, Delhi, Kolkata, Bodhgaya and Bagdogra; Bhutan Airlines is the private carrier. [CONFIRM CURRENT ROUTES]

All flights use Visual Flight Rules, so there are no night flights or flights in poor visibility. Weather delays are common: book flexible tickets and allow at least 24 hours for onward connections. On a clear day you'll see 8 of the world's 10 highest peaks, including Everest (8,848 m) and Kanchenjunga (8,586 m). Read about the flight →

By road

Phuentsholing, on the border with West Bengal, is the main land entry point, reachable from Kolkata or Kathmandu. Samdrup Jongkhar in the east, about 3 hours from Guwahati (Assam), is another border town.

Money & phones

Currency

The Ngultrum (Nu.) is pegged 1:1 to the Indian Rupee, which is also legal tender. [CHECK WHICH LARGE INR NOTES ARE CURRENTLY ACCEPTED]

Cards & cash

ATMs in the main towns accept Visa and Mastercard, and most hotels and craft shops take cards. US dollars are widely accepted; banks give better rates than hotels.

Phones

International direct dialling is available, and prepaid local SIM cards are easy to buy.

Tipping

Optional, and entirely up to you based on the service you receive.

Health & insurance

No vaccinations are required, except yellow fever or cholera if you're arriving from an affected area (at least 10 days before arrival). We strongly recommend travel insurance covering medical emergencies, life cover and lost baggage, and, if you're trekking, high-altitude evacuation. [ADD ALTITUDE ADVICE FROM YOUR TREKKING TEAM]

Bhutan is a very safe country for travellers, including solo and women travellers. Your guide and driver are with you throughout.

Etiquette & dress code

  • At dzongs and temples, wear long sleeves and long trousers or skirts, and remove hats. [ADD ANY SPECIFIC DRESS RULES YOUR GUIDES ENFORCE]
  • Photography is usually not allowed inside temples and dzong shrine rooms. Your guide will tell you where it's fine.
  • Walk clockwise around chortens, prayer wheels and temples.
  • Ask before photographing people, especially monks and festival dancers.
  • At festivals, never walk across the dance ground.

Travel responsibly

  • Choose environmentally certified hotels and lodges.
  • Avoid single-use plastic: bring a reusable bottle, bags and containers.
  • Keep your distance from wildlife and stay on marked trails.
  • Buy local products, eat at local restaurants and travel with local guides.
  • Respect customs and religious sites.
